01 The commitments

Five things we actually sign.

Written here the way we'd say them out loud. The contract version says the same things with more commas.

01
Ownership

Everything is yours from the first commit

Work happens in your GitHub organisation and your cloud accounts, not ours. Code, infrastructure, credentials and documentation are yours the whole way through — so there is no handover ceremony at the end, because we were never holding anything.

100% of code & IP — yours
02
People

The engineers you meet are the engineers you get

Your proposal names individuals, not roles. They're the people on your discovery call and the people in your standups. Any change of personnel goes through you, in writing, before it happens.

Named in the proposal
03
Price

The number comes before the work

A fixed price or a transparent hourly rate, in writing within 48 hours of the first call. Scope changes get quoted and approved before work continues — nothing is billed that you haven't agreed to first.

48 hrs to a written price
04
Visibility

You watch progress — you don't request updates

A working demo every week, plus standing access to the repository and the board from day one. A bad week shows up in that week's demo, never as a surprise at the deadline.

Weekly working demo
05
Exit

Leaving should take one conversation

Because everything already lives in your accounts, there is nothing to migrate, export or unwind. Every build includes 30 days of post-launch support; a retainer after that is an option, never a dependency.

30 days support included

These five are carried into every engagement agreement we sign. If any of them matter to you, ask us to point at the clause on the call.

03 Straight answers

The questions people ask on call two.

Answered here instead, so you can skip a meeting.

Q1Where are you actually based?

Gobichettipalayam, Tamil Nadu, India. We keep deliberate overlap with US and UK business hours for standups, demos and anything urgent — async by default, with a scheduled live window every working day.

Q2What does an engagement cost?

A fixed price or a transparent hourly rate, in writing within 48 hours of the first call. If budget and scope don't line up you'll hear it on that call — including when the honest number is smaller than you planned to spend.

Q3We already have a team. Does this work?

It's most of what we do. Our engineers work inside your repository and your rituals rather than alongside them, and step out on 30 days' notice once you've hired. Making ourselves unnecessary is the goal.

Q4What if the project goes wrong?

You'll know in that week's demo rather than at the deadline, and because the code and infrastructure are already yours, changing course — with us or without us — costs a conversation, not a migration.

Test every one of them on a call.

Bring this page with you and hold us to it. If the honest answer is “don't hire us,” that's the answer you'll get.
